EOS Demo Journey — 60-Minute Call Guide
AE + SE  ·  Internal use only  ·  AppViewX 2026
AE
SE
Both
Comfort→ Curiosity→ Discomfort→ Urgency→ Relief→ Commitment Each block moves the customer one step along this arc
01
Intros and Context Setting
0:00–0:05
BOTH
Comfort→Comfort
AE opens
"Before we get into anything — I want to make sure this is actually useful for you today, not just a product tour. I'm going to ask a few questions first, and based on what you tell us, [SE] is going to show you the parts most relevant to where you are right now. Sound good?"
SE intro (30 sec)
"I'll be showing you the platform today. I [built / lead engineering on] a lot of what you're going to see — feel free to ask anything technical. No question is too deep."
"I built this" is a credibility anchor no competitor can match in the room.
02
Pre-Demo Discovery
0:05–0:15
AE
Comfort→Curiosity
AE leads entirely. SE silent unless a direct technical question surfaces that only SE can answer.
"Let me understand your world before we open anything. Five or six questions — then we'll get into the platform."
Agent landscape
"What AI tools or agents are your teams actively using today — that you know of?"
"Are those centrally approved, or are teams spinning things up on their own?"
"Are developers using coding assistants? Copilot, Cursor, Claude Code?"
"Any SaaS agents — Agentforce, Copilot Studio, ServiceNow?"
"Are any teams building custom agents or calling LLM APIs directly?"
Security ownership
"Who owns AI agent risk today — centralized in security, or distributed across business units?"
"When a dev spins up a coding agent, what's the approval process before it gets access? Is there one?"
"Does security know when an agent gets deployed — real time, or after the fact?"
Visibility gaps + timeline
"How confident are you in your current agent inventory count?"
"Would your SOC get an alert if an agent accessed something it shouldn't?"
"What's driving the focus right now — compliance deadline, audit, board ask?"
"Evaluating other tools? Is this budgeted for this year?"
Bridge line (most important sentence of the call): "Based on what you told us — [repeat back 2-3 things] — [SE], let's start with [entry point from path selector]."
Handoff: AE names entry point explicitly. SE takes screen. No ambiguity.
03
Act 1 — Integrations
0:15–0:18
SE + AE
Comfort→Comfort
SE
"Everyone asks how hard this is to set up. The answer is: not hard. Here's our integrations page. Everything you mentioned — [their platforms] — native connectors. Setting one up looks like this... Once connected, discovery starts automatically."
AE close questions
"Which platforms did you recognize from your own environment?"
"What's your MDM situation — Intune, JAMF, something else?"
File answers — they refine what SE shows in Act 2.
Handoff: AE: "[SE], let's go into discovery."
04
Act 2 — Discover
0:18–0:28
SE + AE
Curiosity→Discomfort
Emotional center of the demo. SE drives product. AE watches faces and jumps in when someone reacts.
SE setup
"What you're looking at is every AI agent running across the organization — not just the ones IT approved, not just the ones security knows about. All of them."
Pause. Let them look at the number. Do not rush past it.
"Before we deployed, the team thought they had [X] agents. We found [Y]."
Walk sequence — in this order:
1
Total agent count — always bigger than expected
2
Risk distribution: critical / high / medium
3
One unowned agent — the moment. Pause on it.
4
Full AIBOM: owner, MCP servers, credentials, LLM, permission scope
"This agent is connected to three MCP servers. Read/write to code repos. Nobody owns it."
SE: Do not keep talking. Let the silence do the work.
AE — jump in at any lean-forward moment
"Does this match what you expected, or does something surprise you?"
"That agent has no owner. Who'd be accountable in your org?"
"Do you have visibility today into what MCP servers your agents are connected to?"
"So that's the inventory problem. Of everything running, what's actually dangerous? [SE], show them the risk layer."
Handoff: AE bridge is the cue. SE moves to risk scoring.
05
Act 3 — Govern and Risk
0:28–0:38
SE + AE
Discomfort→Urgency
Pick 2–3 findings max. Lead with what maps to their stated pain. Do not show everything.
SE setup
"Every agent gets continuously scored across five risk dimensions. We tell you exactly why it's flagged and what to do about it."
Finding selection — based on discovery answers:
They said...Lead with this finding
"Don't know what agents can access"Unowned agent with standing privileges
"Worried about credential exposure"Shared credential across agents
"Devs connecting to external tools"Unauthorized MCP server connection
"Compliance review coming up"SOC 2 / NIST AI RMF compliance gap
"Concerned about data exposure"Over-permissioned scope on sensitive data
For each finding: what was found → why it's a risk → what EOS does (Jira auto-ticket, lifecycle workflow)
AE compliance bridge (after finding 1)
"Is your SOC 2 auditor asking about AI agent access controls yet? What are you telling them?"
AE urgency question (after finding 2–3)
"If one of these findings existed in your environment tonight — not here, in yours — how would you know?"
Designed to produce silence or discomfort. Both are good. Do not fill the silence immediately.
"We've seen what exists and what's risky. Can EOS actually stop something, or just tell you about it? [SE], enforcement layer."
Handoff: AE bridge is the cue. SE moves to runtime enforcement.
06
Act 4 — Secure and Runtime
0:38–0:45
SE + AE
Urgency→Relief
AE sets up PocketOS before SE touches the screen. One policy, one blocked action. Keep it under 7 minutes.
AE — PocketOS setup (before SE shows anything)
"Before [SE] shows how this works — a few weeks ago, a developer at PocketOS was using Cursor with Claude Opus on a database task. The agent interpreted its instructions broadly and deleted the entire production database — and all its backups. Nothing was in place to stop it before it acted."
Pause. Then: "What [SE] is about to show you is the layer that would have prevented that."
SE — runtime demo
"This policy says this agent — regardless of what it's told — cannot modify or delete anything in production. Watch what happens when it tries."
Trigger blocked action. Show blocked event with full attribution. 60 seconds max on this.
"That action never happened. Blocked before it executed. Logged with full attribution for your SOC team."
AE close questions
"Does your team have anything today that blocks an agent in real time — not just logs it after?"
"What's the blast radius if one of your coding agents had prod access and did what that Cursor agent did?"
That second question is the bridge into the advance. Let them answer fully.
07
The Advance
0:45–0:55
AE
Relief→Commitment
AE owns entirely. SE silent. Not closing on a POV — closing on acknowledgment the problem is real and worth solving.
"Let me stop the product there. I'd rather make sure what we showed you actually connected to your world."
Q1
"Of everything we walked through — what's the finding or capability most relevant to where you are right now?"
Let them answer fully. Their answer tells you what landed and what didn't.
Q2
"Is this something your team is actively trying to solve, or more on the radar for later?"
"Actively solving" = deal. "On the radar" = timing problem to probe further.
Q3
"What's the right next step from your side to keep the momentum going?"
Put ownership on them. If they can't answer: "What would need to be true for this to become a priority?"
08
Buffer and Wrap
0:55–1:00
BOTH
"We'll send [specific thing] by [specific day]. I'll follow up with a calendar invite for [next step they named]."
End on time. Never run over. AE summarizes. SE commits to any technical follow-up.
↓
Two-Person Rules
→
AE never touches the product. If you have a question about the screen, ask SE — not the customer.
→
SE never sells. Commercial questions go to AE. "That's a great question for [AE]." Full stop.
→
AE watches faces, SE watches the screen. A lean-in or side glance is a signal — name it with a question.
→
Dead air is always the AE's problem to fill. Jump in with a question. Don't let silence die on SE.
→
Handoffs are always AE → SE. SE never throws it back unprompted.
→
Bridge lines are the AE's job. Every act transition connects back to something from discovery.
