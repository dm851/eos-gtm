02.1 — Ideal Customer Profile
Signal 01
AI agents already in production
Engineers are actively using Claude Code, Cursor, GitHub Copilot, or Agentforce. The problem is live — not theoretical. SDLC agents are the fastest path in: developers adopted before security caught up.
Strongest signalClaude CodeCursorAgentforce

CISO under board pressure on AI governance
80% of security leaders fear identity and data breaches from AI. If the CISO is being asked "what's your AI security posture?" — they have the pain and the budget motivation to fix it.
Compliance pressureSOC 2NIST AI RMFEU AI Act

Multiple agent platforms, fragmented visibility
Running more than two agent platforms with no single pane of glass. IT and security teams don't have visibility into what's running. "I don't know what I don't know" — our strongest opening line.

02.2 — Buying Personas
Champion
Head of Engineering / CTO
The agent adopter. Their team already runs Claude Code, Cursor, or Agentforce. They feel the operational risk but don't have a security solution. They bring EOS in and need a story to take upstairs to the CISO.
"We've standardized on Claude Code but I don't know what it's touching in prod."
Economic Buyer
CISO
The board is asking about AI security posture. They can't answer. They need visibility, an audit trail, and runtime controls before an incident forces the conversation. They fund EOS but rarely discover it themselves.
"I need to know what our agents are doing and be able to show an auditor."
Technical Evaluator
Head of Product Security / IT Security Lead
They evaluate technical fit. They care about integrations, log output, and platform coverage. Win them and they become internal advocates who push the deal up to the economic buyer.
"How do you plug in — are you a gateway, a SIEM feed, or something else?"
Influencer
VP of AI / Head of AI Enablement
They want agents at scale. EOS isn't a blocker — it's the prerequisite that unlocks responsible adoption. Frame it as what lets them move faster, not what slows them down.
"Governance is the thing holding back our AI adoption."
The tension to exploit
Engineering wants to move fast. Security needs to govern. EOS is the bridge — it gives engineering the freedom to adopt agents at scale and gives the CISO the controls to say yes. That positioning lands with both sides of the table simultaneously.
02.3 — Why They Buy
Question 01
Why do anything?
The pain of doing nothing is greater than the friction of buying.
Agents are already running — accessing sensitive files, production systems, and credentials with permissions never designed for them. The PocketOS incident: a Cursor agent deleted an entire production database using credentials it shouldn't have had. Average breach cost $4.9M. SOC 2 auditors are asking for agent activity logs that don't exist yet.
$4.9M avg breach costPocketOS incidentSOC 2 audit exposure80% of CISOs fear AI identity breaches
Question 02
Why EOS?
Discovery alone isn't enough. Runtime enforcement is the differentiator.
Most competitors stop at visibility — they tell you what agents exist. EOS goes further: SDK-based runtime enforcement actively blocks an agent from accessing files, credentials, or systems it's not supposed to touch. In real time. MCP gateway adds just-in-time access. Audit trail satisfies compliance teams. Built on AppViewX's 10-year machine identity foundation — not a startup with a dashboard.
Runtime enforcementSDK-based controlMCP gateway10yr machine identity foundation
Question 03
Why now?
The window between agents going live and the first incident is closing fast.
Developers are ahead of security — already standardized on Claude Code, Cursor, and Agentforce. Every week without governance is another week of expanding attack surface. EU AI Act is live. SOC 2 requirements are evolving. The category is forming now — companies that govern agents early will define the standard.
Agents already in prodEU AI Act liveCategory forming nowFirst mover advantage
02.4 — Discovery Framework
Situation — understand their landscape
Q"Walk me through your AI agent journey — what are your teams actively using today?"
Q"Which teams adopted agents first — engineering, or has it spread across the org?"
Q"How many agent platforms are you running — is there a standard or is it decentralized?"
Problem — surface the visibility gap
Q"Do you have a complete inventory of every agent running in your environment right now?"
Q"When a developer connects an MCP server — does your security team know?"
Q"If I asked what credentials your coding agents have access to, could you tell me?"
Implication — connect to business risk
Q"Have you heard of the PocketOS incident — where a Cursor agent deleted a production database using credentials it shouldn't have accessed?"
Q"If your SOC 2 auditor asked for an activity log of everything your agents accessed last quarter, what would you show them?"
Q"What's your plan if an agent accesses something it's not supposed to — how do you detect and stop it in real time?"
Qualification — MEDDPICC checkpoints
MHow many developers use coding agents? How many platforms in scope? What would a credential exposure incident cost?
EWho owns the AI security budget — CISO, engineering, or a new AI governance initiative?
PWhat happens if this isn't solved by year end — is there a specific audit, incident, or deadline?
For the CISO
→"How is AI agent usage showing up in your security program — formal initiative or still ad hoc?"
→"What's your board asking about AI risk, and how are you answering it right now?"
→"Are agents covered in your SOC 2 or ISO audit scope — or is that still a gap?"
For the Head of Engineering / CTO
→"Have you standardized on one coding agent, or are developers using whatever they prefer?"
→"When you deploy Claude Code or Cursor — what controls do you have over what it can access?"
→"How does your security team feel about the pace of AI adoption in engineering right now?"
